Sermon 192 Summary

Pahankanuwa Sermon #192 Summary

1. Overview

Sermon Title / Number: Pahankanuwa Sermon #192 (Paticca Samuppada Sermon 10)

Primary Theme: The dependent arising (paṭicca-samuppanna) of Kamma, its origin in contact (phassa) and intention (cetanā), a critique of the traditional temporal (three-lifetime) interpretation of Dependent Arising, the distinction between mundane Right View (kammassakatā sammādiṭṭhi) and Supramundane Right View, and the final liberation achieved through the stilling of all preparations (sabba-saṅkhāra-samatha).

2. Background Story (Nidāna-Kathā)

The Protagonist: Arahant Venerable Mahinda Thera, King Devānampiyatissa, and the people of ancient Sri Lanka (commemorated on Poson Poya); alongside historical encounters involving the Buddha, the Brahmin youth Subha, and the disciples of Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta (such as Dīgatapassi).

The Interaction: On Poson Poya, Ven. Mahinda established the Buddha's dispensation in Sri Lanka by preaching discourses such as the Devadūta Sutta (to instil moral dread of Samsāric suffering) and the Cūla Hatthipadopama Sutta (appealing to intellect and virtue). In the canonical background, Subha questioned the Buddha regarding the wide disparities in human lifespans, health, beauty, wealth, and wisdom.

The Disaster / Turning Point: The existential plight of beings trapped in Samsāra: driven by unwholesome roots (lobha, dosa, moha), misled by wrong views (e.g., Jain fatalism or total moral denial), and subjected to extreme physical and mental suffering across low realms (such as hells and petas).

The Result / Resolution: Ven. Mahinda successfully established the Right View in Sri Lanka. The Buddha clarified that kamma is neither fixed past determinism nor fortuitous, but arise dependently on contact (phassa). By cultivating kammassakatā sammādiṭṭhi, beings secure favorable births; by realizing the Supramundane Path, they achieve the cessation of contact (phassa-nirodha), the stilling of all preparations (sabba-saṅkhāra-samatha), and freedom from Samsāra.

3. Core Dhamma Points Emphasized

1. The Seed Simile: Dynamics of Unskilful vs. Skilful Actions

Deeds born of greed, hate, and delusion (lobha, dosa, moha) act like intact seeds sown in fertile soil moistened by craving (sineha), growing into renewed Samsāric existence. Conversely, deeds born of non-greed, non-hate, and non-delusion (alobha, adosa, amoha) act like seeds burned to ashes and swept away by wind or water—their net function is the exhaustion and eradication of unwholesome roots, leading to the cessation of Samsāra.

2. Sixfold Analysis of Kamma (Nibbedhikapariyāya Sutta)

The Buddha provides a complete breakdown of kamma:

Definition: Intention is kamma (cetanāham bhikkhave kammaṁ vadāmi).

Source/Origin: Contact (phassa).

Diversity (vemattatā): Experienced across different realms (hell, animal, ghost, human, deva).

Result: Experienced in this life, the next birth, or subsequent births.

Cessation: The cessation of contact (phassa-nirodha).

Path to Cessation: The Noble Eightfold Path.

3. Primacy of Mind over Body (Manokamma)

Refuting the Nigaṇṭha (Jain) view—which gave precedence to physical action (kāyakamma) and advocated extreme self-mortification (nijjarā) to burn off past kamma (sabbaṁ pubbekatahetu)—the Buddha established the primacy of mental action (manokamma) rooted in intention (Mano pubbaṅgamā dhammā).

4. Refutation of the Tetralemma of Pleasure and Pain

The Buddha refutes the four contemporary extreme views regarding suffering: (1) self-made (sayaṁ kataṁ), (2) other-made (paraṁ kataṁ), (3) both, or (4) fortuitous (adhicca-samuppanna). Instead, he clarifies that kamma and its results are dependently arisen (paṭicca-samuppanna), specifically dependent on contact (phassaṁ paṭicca).

5. Critique of the Three-Lifetime Interpretation of Paṭicca Samuppāda

The sermon challenges later commentarial treatises (such as the Visuddhimagga) that divide the 12 links across past, present, and future lifetimes. Ven. Nanananda emphasizes that all 12 links operate in the present moment, centered on the vortical interplay (vaṭṭa) between consciousness (viññāṇa) and name-and-form (nāma-rūpa).

6. Canonical Re-Definition of Saṅkhāra and *Viññāṇa*

In the Vibhaṅga Sutta, the Buddha defines saṅkhāra not as past kamma, but as the three present preparations: in-and-out breathing (kāyasaṅkhāra), thinking and pondering (vacīsaṅkhāra), and perception and feeling (cittasaṅkhāra). Likewise, viññāṇa is defined as the six sensory consciousnesses (chayime viññāṇakāyā), not merely a past-resultant re-linking consciousness (paṭisandhi viññāṇa).

7. Distinction Between Saṅkhāra and *Abhisaṅkhāra*

Basic preparations (saṅkhāra) are fundamental bodily and mental activities (like breathing and thinking). Special volitional preparations (abhisaṅkhāra) are driven by egotism and intention: meritorious (puññābhisaṅkhāra), demeritorious (apuññābhisaṅkhāra), and imperturbable (āneñjābhisaṅkhāra). While special preparations shape Samsāric rebirths, all preparations must eventually be stilled for liberation.

8. The Two Levels of Right View (Sammā Diṭṭhi)

Mundane Right View (Kammassakatā Sammādiṭṭhi): The 10-fold view recognizing moral causality, gifts, this world/next world, parents, and enlightened recluses. It prevents rebirth in bad bournes and leads to heavenly realms.

Supramundane Right View (Ariyo Aṭṭhaṅgiko Maggo): Direct insight into the Four Noble Truths, transcending personhood, personal agency, and preparations to achieve total deliverance (bhava-nirodha).

9. Old Kamma vs. New Kamma (Purāna & Nava Kamma)

The six internal sense spheres (cakkhu, sota, ghāna, jivhā, kāya, mano) constitute "Old Kamma"—they are specially prepared (abhisaṅkhātaṁ) and intentioned (abhisañcetayitaṁ) results to be experienced. Present intentions constitute "New Kamma." Liberation involves realizing the cessation of the six sense spheres (saḷāyatana-nirodha), stepping from the prepared (saṅkhāta) into the Unprepared (asaṅkhāta).

10. The Iceberg Simile: Deeper Layers of Preparations

Outward actions (abhisaṅkhāra) represent only the visible portion of an iceberg. Beneath the surface lie deeper preparations: kāyasaṅkhāra (breath), vacīsaṅkhāra (sub-vocal thought/pondering), and cittasaṅkhāra (perception and feeling). True spiritual progress involves appeasing these progressively, culminating in the cessation of perception and feeling (saññā-vedayita-nirodha).

11. Distinction Between Merit (Puñña) and the Skilful (Kusala)

Puñña refers to conventional meritorious deeds yields pleasant samsaric results (the 10 puññakiriya-vatthu). Kusala (skilful) relates directly to mental transformation and the roots of non-greed, non-hate, and non-delusion. In its ultimate supramundane sense, being kusala means relinquishing both merit and demerit (puññapāpapahīna) to transcend Samsāra altogether.
