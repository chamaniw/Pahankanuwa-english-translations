Sermon 185 Summary

Pahankanuwa Sermon #185 Summary

1. Overview

Sermon Title / Number: Pahankanuwa Sermon #185 (Paṭicca Samuppāda Sermon 3: The Law of Dependent Arising)

Primary Theme: Analyzing the deep riddle of the "ocean" and "abyss" from the Ekamūla Sutta, demonstrating the reciprocal interdependence between consciousness (viññāṇa) and name-and-form (nāma-rūpa), clarifying the two aspects of contact (phassa), and highlighting the timeless, immediate (sandiṭṭhiko, akāliko) nature of Dependent Arising over commentarial multi-life interpretations.

2. Background Story (Nidāna-Kathā)

The Protagonist: Venerable Ānanda and the Buddha (referencing the framework of the Mahā Nidāna Sutta of the Dīgha Nikāya).

The Interaction: Venerable Ānanda approaches the Buddha at Kammāssadamma in the Kuru country and remarks that although the Law of Dependent Arising is deep and profound, it appears to him as clear as clear.

The Disaster / Turning Point: The Buddha immediately admonishes Venerable Ānanda ("Do not say so, Ānanda!"), warning him that it is precisely through not understanding and not penetrating this deep Dhamma that beings become entangled like a matted bird's nest or woven grass (muñja-babbaja), unable to pass beyond states of woe, downfall, and the round of birth and death.

The Result / Resolution: The Buddha delivers a profound catechism detailing how consciousness (viññāṇa) and name-and-form (nāma-rūpa) are mutually dependent, how contact (phassa) arises from them, and how emancipation is realized when consciousness becomes unestablished (appatiṭṭhita-viññāṇa).

3. Core Dhamma Points Emphasized

1. Sutta-Based Decoding of the Ekamūla Riddle Verse: Rather than relying on late commentarial shortcuts, Ven. Nanananda decodes the riddle verse (Ekamūlaṁ dvirāvaṭṭaṁ...) using explicit canonical references:

One root (ekamūlaṁ): Contact (phassa, per the Phassamūlaka Sutta).

Turning twice (dvirāvaṭṭaṁ): See-sawing between pleasure and pain due to ignorance of the exit from painful feeling (Salla Sutta).

Three stains (timalaṁ): Lust (rāga), hate (dosa), and ignorance (avijjā).

Five expanses (pañcapattharaṁ): The five arenas of sense-pleasures (pañca kāmaguṇā).

Ocean with twelve eddies (samuddaṁ dvādasāvaṭṭaṁ): The six internal and six external sense spheres.

The abyss (pātālaṁ): Painful bodily feelings (dukkhānaṁ vedanānaṁ adhivacanaṁ).

2. Critique of Commentarial Over-Simplification: The commentary interprets the root (mūla), the ocean (samudda), and the abyss (pātāla) all homogenously as craving (taṇhā). Ven. Nanananda demonstrates that this approach loses the specific structural coherence and rich metaphorical imagery of the canonical Suttas.

3. The Twofold Aspects of Contact (Phassa): Drawing from the Mahā Nidāna Sutta, contact is analyzed into two essential components:

Verbal/Designation impression (adhivacana-samphassa): Derived from the name-group (nāmakāya—feeling, perception, intention, contact, attention).

Resistance impression (paṭigha-samphassa): Derived from the form-group (rūpakāya—the four great primaries). Contact in the sentient world functions like "Siamese twins" born of nāma and rūpa, requiring both recognition and impact.

4. Reciprocal Interdependence of Viññāṇa and Nāma-rūpa: Consciousness and name-and-form exist in a mutual vortex or whirlpool (ettāvatā vaṭṭaṁ vaṭṭati). Consciousness requires nāma-rūpa to gain a footing (patiṭṭhā) and grow in the womb; conversely, nāma-rūpa cannot grow, mature, or be designated without viññāṇa.

5. The Six Oceans of Sense Experience: The six sense faculties (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind) are described as oceans (samudda), sense objects as waves (vega), and emotional entanglements as sea-demons (rakkhasa). An Arahant is one who withstands these sensory waves and stands safely on dry ground.

6. Refutation of the Three-Life Interpretation: Texts such as the Titthāyatana Sutta (describing descent into the womb via six elements) are frequently misread as supporting a three-life timeline. Ven. Nanananda clarifies that Paṭicca Samuppāda is an invariable, timeless (akāliko) law operating directly in present experience (sandiṭṭhiko), where feeling (vedanā) directly branches into the Four Noble Truths.

7. The Duality of 'Thisness' (Itthatta) and 'Otherwiseness' (Aññathābhāva): Driven by craving (taṇhādutiyo puriso) and the four perversions (vipallāsā), the worldling clings to existence as a fixed "thisness" (itthatta), which perpetually collapses into "otherwiseness" (aññathābhāva), sustaining the samsāric vortex.

8. Unestablished Consciousness (Appatiṭṭhita-viññāṇa / Anidassana-viññāṇa): Emancipation is achieved when consciousness no longer finds a footing in nāma-rūpa. This state of non-manifestative consciousness (anidassana-viññāṇa) cuts off future birth, decay, and death.

9. Crossing the Abyss and Unfelt Bliss (Avedayita-Sukha): The Arahant crosses the "abyss" of painful bodily feelings by realizing Nibbāna, which Ven. Sāriputta describes as supreme bliss precisely because it is free from all feeling (avedayita sukha), abiding in concentration that is signless (animitta), undirected (appaṇihita), and void (suññā).
