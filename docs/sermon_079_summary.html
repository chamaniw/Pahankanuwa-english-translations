Pahankanuwa Sermon #79 Summary

Sermon #79 Summary

1. Overview

- Sermon Title / Number: Pahankanuwa Sermon #79 (Pahankaṇuwa Sinhala Audio Dhamma Sermon No. 134)

- Primary Theme: The profound inner and cosmic significance of setting in motion the Wheel of Dhamma (Dhammacakkappavattana), its experiential reality within the mind-stream through the Four Noble Truths and Dependent Arising (paṭicca-samuppāda), and the authentic process of internal transformation leading to Stream-entry (sotāpatti) and ultimate liberation (Nibbāna).

---

2. Background Story (Nidāna-Kathā)

- The Protagonist: The Supreme Buddha (Gotama), Sahampati Brahma, the group of five ascetics (Pañcavaggiyā, led by Kondañña), and secondary figures including Alara Kalama, Uddaka Ramaputta, and the Ajivaka Upaka.

- The Interaction: Following Supreme Enlightenment, while dwelling at the foot of the Ajapala Banyan tree, the Buddha considered the extreme depth of the discovered Dhamma—specifically Dependent Arising (paṭicca-samuppāda) and Nibbāna—and hesitated to preach to a world bound by craving (taṇhā). Aware of this, Sahampati Brahma descended from the Brahma realm, paid homage, and thrice entreated the Buddha to preach the Dhamma, presenting the simile of an onlooker standing on a rocky peak viewing worldlings below, and noting that there are beings with little dust in their eyes (apparajakkhakā).

- The Disaster / Turning Point: The crisis of potential world destruction had the Buddha chosen silence; the tragic loss of former accomplished teachers Alara Kalama and Uddaka Ramaputta who had just passed away; and the deep skepticism and open disrespect of the five ascetics at Isipatana Deer Park, who wrongly believed that Gotama had abandoned his spiritual quest and reverted to a life of luxury (bāhulika).

- The Result / Resolution: The Buddha surveyed the world with the Buddha-Eye (buddha-cakkhu) using the simile of the lotus pond (uppalādiniyā upamā), saw varying capacities of beings, and declared the opening of the doors to the Deathless (apārutā tesaṁ amatassa dvārā). Upon reaching Varanasi, He subdued the five ascetics using the miracle of truth (saccā-pāṭihāriya), causing them to listen receptively. He delivered the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta, introducing the Middle Path (majjhimā paṭipadā) and the Four Noble Truths. Ascetic Kondañña attained the Fruit of Stream-entry (sotāpatti-phala), realizing “yaṁ kiñci samudayadhammaṁ sabbaṁ taṁ nirodhadhammaṁ”, and the Wheel of Dhamma rolled upwards through all devalokas to the Akanittha Brahma world.

---

3. Core Dhamma Points Emphasized

1. The Buddha's Hesitation and Sahampati Brahma’s Request (*Brahma-yācanā*):

The Dhamma discovered by the Buddha is deep (gambhīra), hard to see, peaceful, refined, beyond logic, and directly concerned with Dependent Arising (paṭicca-samuppāda). Brahma Sahampati’s supplication highlights that without the preaching of this pure Dhamma, the world faces spiritual destruction.

2. Surveying the World with the Buddha-Eye (*Buddha-cakkhu*) & The Lotus Pond Simile:

The Buddha perceived the varying spiritual faculties (indriya) and levels of defilements among beings using the lotus pond metaphor: lotuses submerged in water, those level with the surface, and those standing clear above the water undefiled.

3. The Miracle of Truth (*Saccā-pāṭihāriya*):

To overcome the rigid cynicism of the five ascetics, the Buddha did not use psychic powers but appealed to undeniable truth, asking: "Monks, have I ever spoken to you in this manner before?" This truthful assertion instantly subdued their arrogance and opened their ears to the Dhamma.

4. Bypassing the Two Extremes (*Antā*) via the Middle Path (*Majjhimā Paṭipadā*):

The Buddha condemned both kāmasukhallikānuyoga (immersion in sensual pleasures; ignoble and unbeneficial) and attakilamathānuyoga (self-mortification; painful and ignoble). He presented the Middle Path—the Noble Eightfold Path (ariya aṭṭhaṅgika magga)—which gives rise to vision (cakkhukaraṇī) and knowledge (ñāṇakaraṇī).

5. The Four Noble Truths (*Caturāriya-sacca*):

- Dukkha-sacca: Summarized as the five aggregates of grasping (pañcupādānakkhandhā).

- Samudaya-sacca: Craving (taṇhā), which is threefold—sensual craving (kāma-taṇhā), craving for existence/becoming (bhava-taṇhā, aligned with eternalism/sassata-diṭṭhi), and craving for non-existence/annihilation (vibhava-taṇhā, aligned with annihilationism/uccheda-diṭṭhi).

- Nirodha-sacca: The remainderless fading away and cessation of craving (tassāyeva taṇhāya asesavirāganirodho).

- Magga-sacca: The Noble Eightfold Path from Right View (sammā-diṭṭhi) to Right Concentration (sammā-samādhi).

6. The Three Rounds and Twelve Aspects (*Tiparivaṭṭa Dvādasākāra*):

The Wheel of Dhamma consists of three revolutions applied to each of the Four Truths:

- Sacce ñāṇa: Recognizing the truth (e.g., "This is Dukkha").

- Kicca ñāṇa: Knowing what must be done (e.g., pariññeyyaṁ—to be fully understood; pahātabbaṁ—to be abandoned; sacchikātabbaṁ—to be realized; bhāvetabbaṁ—to be developed).

- Kata ñāṇa: Recognizing completion (e.g., pariññātaṁ, pahīnaṁ, sacchikataṁ, bhāvitaṁ).

The Buddha claimed supreme enlightenment only when this twelvefold knowledge and vision (ñāṇadassana) became thoroughly purified.

7. Stream-Entry Realization (*Sotāpatti-phala*) and the Eye of Dhamma (*Dhamma-cakkhu*):

Venerable Kondañña's attainment was marked by the arising of the Dhamma-eye: “yaṁ kiñci samudayadhammaṁ sabbaṁ taṁ nirodhadhammaṁ” ("Whatever is of the nature to arise, all that is of the nature to cease"). This concise formula captures the core insight of Dependent Arising (paṭicca-samuppāda)—that the conditioned five aggregates arise due to causes (ignorance) and vanish completely when causes cease.

8. Contrast between Worldly Wheel-Treasure (*Cakka-ratana*) and the Dhamma Wheel (*Dhamma-cakka*):

A Wheel-turning Monarch (Cakkavatti) subdues horizontal, worldly realms non-violently through the five precepts (pañca-sīla). In contrast, the Supreme King of Dhamma (Dhammarāja) rolls the supramundane Wheel of Dhamma vertically—upwards from the earth-dwelling deities to the highest Akanittha Brahma realm—transforming internal mind-streams.

9. Venerable Sariputta as the General of Dhamma (*Dhammasenāpati*):

As confirmed in the Sela Sutta and demonstrated in the Saccavibhaṅga Sutta, Ven. Sariputta rolls the Wheel of Dhamma after the Buddha by providing comprehensive, analytical expositions of the core truths preached by the Master.

10. Correction of Popular Misconceptions Regarding Stream-Entry (*Sotāpanna*):

The Thero clarifies that sota does not mean "ear," but a stream or current (a river flowing unhindered to the ocean of Nibbāna). Stream-entry cannot be defined merely by nominal adherence to the five precepts; it requires a radical internal breakthrough that shatters identity view (sakkāya-diṭṭhi).

11. The Experiential Process of Disillusionment (*Nibbidā*) and Dispassion (*Virāga*):

Authentic Dhamma practice relies on deep insight (vipassanā) into the Three Characteristics (tilakkhaṇa)—contemplating the five aggregates through eleven modes (as impermanent, suffering, a disease, a tumor, a dart, a calamity, an affliction, alien, disintegrating, empty, and non-self). This deep disillusionment (nibbidā) induces dispassion (virāga), leading to automatic relinquishment of grasping, just as one instinctively shakes off a rotting carcass from one's shoulders.

12. Internalizing the Fourfold Turning of the Dhamma Wheel:

The ultimate instruction of the sermon is that practitioners must not treat the Wheel of Dhamma as a mere external symbol or historical event. It must be actively turned within one's own mind-stream across the four stages of awakening (Stream-entry, Once-returning, Non-returning, and Arahatship) to achieve complete cessation of becoming (bhava-nirodha) in this very life.
