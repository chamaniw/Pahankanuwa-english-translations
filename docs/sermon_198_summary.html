Sermon 198 Summary

Pahankanuwa Sermon #198 Summary

Here is the structured scholarly analysis and summary of Pahankanuwa Dhamma Sermon #198.

---

Sermon #198 Summary

1. Overview

Sermon Title / Number: Pahankanuwa Sermon #198 (Paṭicca Samuppāda Sermon 16: The Law of Dependent Arising – Vedanā)

Primary Theme: The deep philosophical and practical contemplation of Feeling (vedanānupassanā) within Dependent Arising (paṭicca samuppāda). The sermon demonstrates how understanding feeling’s contact-conditioned nature (tajjaṁ phassaṁ) and penetrating the ignored middle position (adukkhamasukhā vedanā) breaks the illusion of compactness (ghaṇa-saññā), eradicates latent defilements (anusaya), and leads to emancipation through unestablished consciousness (appatiṭṭhita viññāṇa).

---

2. Background Story (Nidāna-Kathā)

The Protagonist: Venerable Godhika Thera (a dedicated meditative monk striving on Isigili rock), complemented by canonical dialogues featuring Arahant Therī Dhammadinnā & lay disciple Visākha (Cūlavedalla Sutta), and Ven. Nandaka & 500 nuns (Nandakovāda Sutta).

The Interaction: Ven. Godhika practiced intensive meditation in solitude on the slopes of Kālasilā at Isigili Rock, repeatedly attaining temporary spiritual liberation of mind (sāmayika ceto vimutti).

The Disaster / Turning Point: Due to persistent physical illnesses or unfavorable conditions, Ven. Godhika fell away from his samādhi state six consecutive times. Experiencing deep disappointment and recognizing the extreme fragility of conditioned life, he decided to slice his own throat with a sharp razor to attain final liberation before slipping again. Māra attempted to intervene by appealing to the Buddha, hoping to force Godhika into clinging to life.

The Result / Resolution: At the moment of fatal injury, instead of falling into terror or reaching for sensual escape, Ven. Godhika applied intense insight into feeling (vedanānupassanā), severing craving (taṇhā) at its very root. He attained Parinibbāna with an unestablished consciousness (appatiṭṭhitena viññāṇena), leaving Māra searching in vain like a smoky cloud across the ten directions for his rebirth-consciousness.

---

3. Core Dhamma Points Emphasized

1. The Deceptive Nature of Feeling (*Vedanā*):

Feeling is categorized into three types: pleasant (sukhā), unpleasant (dukkhā), and neither-pleasant-nor-unpleasant (adukkhamasukhā).

As declared in the Cūlavedalla Sutta, pleasant feelings are pleasant while they persist, but their change is painful (ṭhitisukhā vipariṇāmadukkhā). Unpleasant feelings are painful while they persist, but their change is pleasant (ṭhitidukkhā vipariṇāmasukhā). Neither-pleasant-nor-unpleasant feeling is pleasant when surveyed with wisdom, but deceptive/unpleasant when surveyed without wisdom.

2. Latent Tendencies (*Anusaya*) Underlying Feelings:

Mindful analysis reveals the three hidden latencies: lust (rāgānusaya) underlies pleasant feeling; aversion/anger (paṭighānusaya) underlies unpleasant feeling; ignorance (avijjānusaya) underlies neither-pleasant-nor-unpleasant feeling.

Exceptions: Special meditative states bypass these latencies—e.g., First Jhāna contains joy/ease without rāgānusaya; the noble yearning for unacquired Arahatship (nekkhammasita domanassa) contains dejection without paṭighānusaya; and the Fourth Jhāna contains neutral feeling without avijjānusaya.

3. The Ignored Middle Position and the Counterparts (*Paṭibhāga*):

Worldlings cycle continuously between the two extremes of pleasure and pain (compared to a ride on a see-saw).

The counterpart of sukhā vedanā is dukkhā vedanā, but the deeper neutral position (adukkhamasukhā) acts as the concealed plateau (illustrated by the simile of deer footprints left on bare land but hidden on a rocky plateau). Surveyed with wisdom, this neutral middle unfolds into true knowledge (vijjā), emancipation (vimutti), and Nibbāna.

4. Craving as the Seamstress (*Sibbani*) in the *Majjhe Sutta*:

Drawing from the Metteyya Pañha, one end is pleasant feeling, the second end is unpleasant feeling, and the middle is neutral feeling.

Craving (taṇhā) acts as a "seamstress" (sibbani), continuously sewing together the two opposite ends of contact/arising of contact while deliberately concealing the middle cessation (nirodha).

5. Specific Contact-Conditioning (*Tajjaṁ Phassaṁ*):

Highlighted in the Phassamūlaka Sutta: "Tajjaṁ tajjaṁ phassaṁ paṭicca tajjā tajjā vedanā uppajjanti..." Specific feelings arise dependent on specific contacts and cease completely when those specific contacts cease.

Wood-Rubbing Simile: Just as heat (usmā) is generated only while two sticks are actively rubbed together and disappears the moment they are separated, feelings cease then and there when sense contact is decoupled.

6. The Three Similes of the *Nandakovāda Sutta*:

Oil Lamp Simile: Demonstrates the impermanence of the six internal sense spheres (ajjhattikāni āyatanāni).

Tree Shadow Simile: Demonstrates the impermanence of the six external sense objects (bāhirāni āyatanāni).

Butcher Severing Cow Nerves Simile: A butcher separates the inner flesh (internal organs) from the outer skin (external objects) by cutting only the joining nerves (nandirāga / delight-and-lust) using a sharp butcher's knife (noble wisdom / ariyo ñāṇa). The enlightened person experiences sense contact without the internal attachment to taste/sensation (rasa-paṭisaṁvedī... no ca rasa-rāga-paṭisaṁvedī).

7. Gradual Maturation of the Nuns (The Full Moon Simile):

Ven. Nandaka repeated the same sermon on two consecutive days. On the 14th day (waxing moon nearly full), the 500 nuns were gladdened but had not fully realized expectations. On the 15th day (full moon), through repeated contemplation of tajjaṁ phassaṁ, all 500 nuns realized spiritual fruits, with the lowest spiritual attainment among them being Stream-Entry (Sotāpanna).

8. Unestablished Consciousness (*Appatiṭṭhita Viññāṇa*) at Death:

The ordinary worldling undergoing death engages in a tug-of-war with Māra, clinging (upādāna) to life and causing rebirth-consciousness to establish (patiṭṭhita viññāṇa) in a new existence (bhava).

Ven. Godhika released all clinging to body, feeling, and life at the critical threshold. By letting go completely, his consciousness remained unestablished (appatiṭṭhita), resulting in immediate Parinibbāna.

9. Dismantling the Perception of Compactness (*Ghaṇa-Saññā*):

Practical advice for meditators facing severe physical pain during cross-legged sitting (padmāsana). By applying the fourfold effort (caturanga-viriya) and observing painful sensation directly, the illusion of pain as a solid, monolithic block (ghaṇa-saññā) dissolves.

The meditator penetrates pain as a rapidly arising and passing cluster of momentary sensation units (rāsi-saññā), matching the Buddha's simile of rain drops generating short-lived bubbles on water.
