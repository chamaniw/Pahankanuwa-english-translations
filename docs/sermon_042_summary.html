Sermon 042 Summary

Pahankanuwa Sermon #42 Summary

1. Overview

Sermon Title / Number: Pahankanuwa Sermon #42

Primary Theme: Crossing the flood of saṃsāra via the Middle Path (ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo) as illustrated by the simile of the skilled cowherd (Cūḷagopālaka Sutta), featuring the accessibility of liberation for Faith- and Dhamma-followers, the structural mechanics of supramundane concentration (Mahācattārīsaka Sutta), and the analytical distinction between practice (paṭipadā) and path-moment (magga).

2. Background Story (Nidāna-Kathā)

The Protagonist: The Buddha (the supreme, skilled guide) speaking to a group of monks on the banks of the River Ganges near Ukkācelā in the Vajjian country, contrasting an unwise Magadhan cowherd with a wise Magadhan cowherd.

The Interaction: Taking inspiration from a common sight along the Ganges, the Buddha recounts a real historical event (bhūtapubbaṃ) involving two cowherds attempting to drive their herds across the flooded river to Videha during the autumn season.

The Disaster / Turning Point: The foolish cowherd tried to cross his cattle without examining this bank or the further bank, driving them through an improper ford (anotitithena). Consequently, the entire herd was swept away and destroyed by the fierce current. This illustrates relying on unskilled ascetics and brahmins who lack comprehension of this world, the next world, Māra’s realm, and the realm subject to death.

The Result / Resolution: In contrast, the wise cowherd systematically examined both banks and successfully crossed his herd at a proper ford in five successive groups:

1. The bulls/leaders (gopitaro gopariṇāyakā),

2. The strong cattle and cows,

3. The grown heifers and young bulls,

4. The weak calves, and

5. The newborn calf born that very day, urged on by its mother’s protective lowing sound (mātugoravena vuyhamāno).

The Buddha identifies Himself as the skilled cowherd who knows all realms and has opened the door to the Deathless (amatadvāraṃ), guaranteeing that all five categories of His followers—from Arahants down to Faith- and Dhamma-followers—will safely cross the river of saṃsāra to Nibbāna.

3. Core Dhamma Points Emphasized

1. Mapping the Cowherd Simile to the Noble Disciples:

The Buddha maps the five categories of cattle to spiritual attainments in the Sāsana:

Bulls / Leaders: Arahants who have severed all ten fetters (saṃyojana) and destroyed the taints (āsava).

Strong Cattle / Cows: Non-Returners (Anāgāmī) who have severed the five lower fetters (oramabhāgiya-saṃyojana) and are reborn spontaneously (opapātika) in the Pure Abodes (Suddhāvāsa).

Grown Heifers / Young Bulls: Once-Returners (Sakadāgāmī) who have severed the first three fetters and thinned out lust, hatred, and delusion.

Weak Calves: Stream-Enterers (Sotāpanna) who have severed the first three fetters, are no longer subject to lower worlds (avinipātadhammo), and are bound for awakening (niyato sambodhiparāyaṇo) within a maximum of seven births (sattakkhattuparama).

Newborn Calf: Dhamma-followers (Dhammānusārī) and Faith-followers (Saddhānusārī), who cross over with the protective backing of the teaching, just as the calf crosses guided by its mother's lowing.

2. The Solace of Dhammānusārī and Saddhānusārī:

These two types of individuals sit just below Stream-Entry, yet the Buddha offers a profound consolation by affirming that they too are guaranteed to cross over. According to texts like the Alagaddūpama Sutta and Kīṭāgiri Sutta, they are bound for awakening and will not pass away without realizing the fruit of Stream-Entry (sotāpattiphala).

3. Establishment of the Five Spiritual Faculties (Indriya):

Unlike ordinary worldlings (puthujjana), both the Dhammānusārī and Saddhānusārī have established the five spiritual faculties: faith (saddhindriya), energy (viriyindriya), mindfulness (satindriya), concentration (samāhindriya), and wisdom (paññindriya).

In the Dhamma-follower, the faculty of wisdom is dominant; they have reflected on and gained a measured conviction in teachings such as impermanence (paññāya mattaso nijjhānaṃ khamanti).

In the Faith-follower, the faculty of faith is dominant; they proceed through a degree of faith and devotion toward the Tathāgata (saddhāmattaṃ pemamattaṃ).

4. Transcendence of the Worldling Plane (Okkantika Saṃyutta):

The sermon highlights key markers of entering the path of certainty from the Okkantika Saṃyutta:

Okkanto sammatta-niyāmaṃ: They have entered the certainty of correctness/rightness (sammatta).

Sappurisabhūmiṃ okkanto: They have entered the plane of the noble/virtuous ones (sappurisa).

Vītivatto puthujjanabhūmiṃ: They have transcended the plane of the ordinary worldling.

Abhabba: They are completely incapable of committing any unwholesome karma that would cause rebirth in hell, the animal realm, or the ghost realm.

5. The Conditioned Path Leading to the Unconditioned (Aggappasāda Sutta):

Referencing the Aggappasāda Sutta, the Buddha identifies the Noble Eightfold Path as supreme among all conditioned phenomena (saṅkhata). The unique genius of the Buddha’s teaching lies in utilizing a conditioned vehicle (saṅkhata)—the Middle Path—to lead practitioners directly to the Unconditioned (asaṅkhata), Nibbāna.

6. Noble Right Concentration as the Core Path (Mahācattārīsaka Sutta):

Drawing from the Mahācattārīsaka Sutta, Ven. Nanananda explains that the "Path" (magga) fundamentally consists of Noble Right Concentration (ariya sammā-samādhi). The preceding seven factors act as its essential requisites (saparihāraṃ) and supporting conditions (saupanisaṃ).

7. The Twofold Division of Right View (Sammā-Diṭṭhi):

Right View is designated as the forerunner (pubbaṅgamā) and is categorized into two levels:

Mundane Right View: Accompanied by taints (sāsavā), siding with merit (puññabhāgiyā), ripening in worldly acquisitions (upadhivepakkā). It affirms karmic fruit, this world/next world, and spontaneous rebirth to prevent fall into miserable realms.

Supramundane Right View: Noble (ariyā), taintless (anāsavā), supramundane (lokuttarā). It manifests during the path-moment as active wisdom (paññā), investigation of states (dhamma-vicaya), and the wisdom faculty (paññindriya).

8. Dynamics and Security of the Supramundane Path-Moment:

In the supramundane path-moment, all eight path factors align simultaneously in a single mind-moment. During this event:

Right View leads, while Right Effort (sammā-vāyāma) and Right Mindfulness (sammā-sati) actively run along (anuparidhāvanti) and revolve around (anuparivattanti) the mind, providing a complete security detail for Noble Right Concentration.

Morality factors (Right Speech, Action, Livelihood) do not require active effort but manifest as perfected abstinences (virati).

9. Immediate Concentration (Ānantarika-Samādhi):

Path-concentration (magga-samādhi) is the exact "immediate concentration" praised in the Ratana Sutta. It is termed ānantarika because the realization of the fruit (phala) follows the path-moment (magga-citta) immediately without any time interval.

10. Reinterpreting "Path" versus "Practice" (Magga vs. Paṭipadā):

Ven. Nanananda clarifies a common doctrinal confusion regarding the "eight noble individuals" (aṭṭha purisapuggalā). Pointing to verses in the Aṅguttara Nikāya (paṭipannā cattāro ca, phale ṭhitā...), he notes that the eight individuals consist of four established in the fruit (phale ṭhitā) and four in the course of practice (paṭipannā), rather than static "path-attainers" (maggaṭṭha). Since the supramundane path (magga) is a single instantaneous mind-moment, practitioners like the Dhammānusārī and Saddhānusārī reside within the course of practice leading to the fruit of Stream-Entry (sotāpattiphala-sacchikiriyāya paṭipanno).
