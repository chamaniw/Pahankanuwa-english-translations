Sermon 201 Summary

Pahankanuwa Sermon #201 Summary

1. Overview

Sermon Title / Number: Pahankanuwa Sermon #201 (Paṭicca Samuppāda Sermon 19)

Primary Theme: The deep doctrinal analysis of the Four Nutriments (cattāro āhārā) within the framework of Dependent Arising (Paṭicca Samuppāda), illustrating how contemplating their arising, ceasing, and the Middle Path leads to disillusionment, unestablished consciousness (appatiṭṭhita viññāṇa), and final liberation (Arahatta).

2. Background Story (Nidāna-Kathā)

The Protagonist: A devoted couple (husband and wife) traveling through an arid desert accompanied by their only beloved son.

The Interaction: The family embarks on a dangerous desert crossing equipped with only a meager supply of provisions.

The Disaster / Turning Point: Halfway through the journey, their food runs out completely. Realizing that all three will perish if nothing is done, the couple reaches a agonizing decision to kill their only child, prepare his flesh, and consume it strictly to survive the remaining journey, all while weeping intensely and lamenting, "Where is our only son?"

The Result / Resolution: The Buddha presents this harrowing narrative (Puttamāṁsa Sutta) to emphasize that gross material food (kabaliṅkārāhāra) must be partaken of purely for survival and maintenance—without lust, sport, or adornment. Grasping this reality frees the noble disciple from sensuality (kāmacchanda), culminating in the attainment of Non-returning (Anāgāmitā).

3. Core Dhamma Points Emphasized

1. Dual Contemplation on Nutriment (Dvayatānupassanā)

The Buddha presents a dual contemplation method focused on nutriment: seeing that all suffering arises conditioned by nutriment (sabbaṁ āhāra paccayā) and that suffering ceases completely with the cessation of nutriment (āhārānaṁ nirodhena). Practicing this vigilance yields either Arahanthood or Non-returning (Anāgāmitā).

2. The Four Types of Nutriment (Cattāro Āhārā)

Life is maintained by four nutriments:

1. Gross material food (kabaliṅkārāhāra)

2. Contact (phassāhāra)

3. Mental intentions/volition (manosañcetanāhāra)

4. Consciousness (viññāṇāhāra)

All four stem directly from craving as their root cause, origin, and source (taṇhā nidānā taṇhā samudayā).

3. Simile of the Son's Flesh (Puttamāṁsa Upamā) & Gross Food

Material food must be treated like consuming a child's flesh in the desert—partaken of solely for body maintenance (kāyassa ṭhitiyā) and survival, not enjoyment or beautification. Fully comprehending material food leads to the destruction of lust for the five sensual pleasures.

4. Simile of the Skinned Cow (Niccammā Gāvī Upamā) & Contact Nutriment

Contact nutriment is compared to a cow with exposed skin wounds attacked by insects wherever she goes (against walls, under trees, in water, or open air). Deeply understanding contact nutriment leads to full comprehension of the three types of feeling (tisso vedanā) and the attainment of Arahanthood.

5. Simile of the Pit of Embers (Aṅgārakāsu Upamā) & Mental Intentions

Volitional intention is likened to a man dragged by two strong men toward a flaming pit of charcoal; though forced forward by craving, his innate desire is to recoil. Understanding mental intention cuts off the three forms of craving: sensual craving (kāma-taṇhā), existence craving (bhava-taṇhā), and non-existence craving (vibhava-taṇhā).

6. Simile of the 300 Dagger Blows (Aḍayaṭi Upamā) & Consciousness Nutriment

Consciousness nutriment is compared to a criminal receiving 300 dagger stabs daily (morning, noon, and night). Understanding consciousness nutriment dismantles the illusion of Name-and-Form (nāma-rūpa), revealing how sensory bombardments (e.g., modern media advertising) constantly strike the unmindful.

7. Eliminating Nutriment via Nutriment (Āhāraṁ Nissāya Āhāro Pahātabbo)

The Middle Path avoids both extremes—total self-mortification/starvation and luxurious self-indulgence. Using the restraint of wisdom (ñāṇa-saṁvara), food is taken mindfully like applying medicine to an open wound (vaṇassa ropanatthāya) or lubricating a cart axle (akkhassabbhañjanaṁ).

8. Contemplation of Elements and Repulsiveness (Dhātu-manasikāra & Paṭikūla-manasikāra)

Almsfood is merely a speck of elements (dhātu-mattako)—earth, water, fire, and wind. Though clean outside, it becomes utterly repulsive (ativiya jigucchanīyo) upon entering the foul body (pūtikāya). Dissecting food and body into basic elements reveals their voidness (suññatā).

9. Unestablished Consciousness (Appatiṭṭhita Viññāṇa) and the Pavilion Simile

When passion (rāga), delight (nandi), and craving (taṇhā) toward the four nutriments are destroyed, consciousness finds no landing place—resembling sunlight passing through a pavilion's windows with no western wall, earth, or water to reflect upon (appatiṭṭhita). Without a foothold, rebirth (āyatiṁ punabbhavābhinibbatti) ceases.

10. Non-manifestative Consciousness (Anidassana Viññāṇa) and Name-and-Form

The liberated mind of an Arahant functions as non-manifestative consciousness (anidassana viññāṇa), wherein the four primary elements and concepts of long/short, coarse/fine, or beautiful/ugly find no footing, causing Name-and-Form to cease without remainder (ettha nāmañca rūpañca asesaṁ uparujjhati).

11. The Unreckonable Arahant (Saṁkhaṁ Nopeti)

Established in the Dhamma and free from influxes (āsavakkhaya), an Arahant experiences taste without lust (rasa-paṭisaṁvedī no ca rasa-rāga-paṭisaṁvedī). Moving in the pasture of the signless (animitta) and void (suññata), their spiritual track is untraceable, like the path of birds in the sky (ākāseva sakuntānaṁ gati tesaṁ durannayā).

12. Right View on Nutriment (Sammādiṭṭhi)

As highlighted by Ven. Sāriputta in the Sammādiṭṭhi Sutta, applying the Four Noble Truths directly to the four nutriments—discerning nutriment, its origin (craving), its cessation (Nibbāna), and the path to its cessation—is a complete self-contained path to Stream-entry and ultimate liberation.
