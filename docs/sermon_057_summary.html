Pahankanuwa Sermon #57 Summary

Sermon #57 Summary

1. Overview

- Sermon Title / Number: Pahankanuwa Sermon #57

- Primary Theme: The life, virtues, and spiritual legacy of Venerable Ānanda Thera as the Treasurer of the Dhamma (Dhammabhaṇḍāgārika); prioritizing spiritual inheritance over material gain (dhamma-dāyāda vs. āmisa-dāyāda); and the systematic development of the five spiritual faculties (indriya) to preserve the Buddha's Dispensation.

---

2. Background Story (Nidāna-Kathā)

- The Protagonist: Venerable Ānanda Thera (alongside earlier temporary attendants such as Nāgasamāla and Meghiya, and the Supreme Buddha).

- The Interaction: For the first twenty years of His ministry, the Buddha lacked a permanent attendant. Temporary attendants often acted on their own whims—such as Nāgasamāla abandoning the Buddha at a crossroads despite repeated warnings, or Meghiya disobeying the Buddha to meditate prematurely in a mango grove. To address this, the Buddha requested the Saṅgha to appoint a permanent attendant.

- The Disaster / Turning Point: While senior Arhats offered their service, the Buddha declined them, choosing Ānanda, who humbly remained silent until asked. Later, at the Buddha’s Parinibbāna, Ānanda faced a major crisis: he was still only a Stream-enterer (Sekha), weeping in grief over his Teacher's passing, while facing the urgent responsibility of holding the entire oral tradition of the Dhamma for the upcoming First Dhamma Council (Saṅgāyana), which required Arhat status for participation.

- The Result / Resolution: Ānanda accepted the attendant post only after securing eight specific boons designed to prevent material suspicion and ensure the acquisition of Dhamma. On the eve of the First Council, through intense mindfulness of the body (kāyagatāsati), Ānanda attained Arahantship in the brief instant as his feet left the ground and before his head touched the pillow. He subsequently recited the discourses starting with "Evaṁ me sutaṁ", safeguarding the Dhamma Treasury for future generations.

---

3. Core Dhamma Points Emphasized

1. The Eight Boons of Attendantship (*Aṭṭha Varā*):

Ven. Ānanda stipulated four refusals (declining fine robes, almsfood, sharing the scented chamber, and private alms invitations) to prevent accusations of serving for material benefit (āmisa). He added four requests (the Buddha attending invitations accepted by Ānanda, arranging visits for seekers, answering his Dhamma doubts, and repeating discourses preached in his absence) to master and preserve the Dhamma.

2. Fivefold Foremost Discipleship (*Etadagga*):

The Buddha declared Ven. Ānanda foremost among disciples in five distinct qualities: great learning (bahussuta), retentive memory (satimanta), analytical grasp/cognitive synthesis (gatimanta), steadfast fortitude (ṭhitimanta), and excellence as an attendant (upaṭṭhāka).

3. Spiritual vs. Material Inheritance (*Dhammadāyāda Sutta*):

The sermon highlights the Buddha’s directive to be heirs of the Dhamma (dhamma-dāyāda), not heirs of material things (āmisa-dāyāda). Rejecting material privileges fosters key ascetic virtues: fewness of wishes (appicchatā), contentment (santuṭṭhitā), easy supportability (subharatā), and effacement of defilements (sallekha).

4. Making the Heart a Scented Chamber (*Gandhakuṭi*):

Although Ven. Ānanda declined to reside in the Buddha’s physical scented chamber, he made his own heart a dwelling for the Buddha’s virtues. True devotion requires internalizing the Dhamma rather than seeking mere external proximity.

5. Four Marvelous Qualities Comparable to a Wheel-Turning Monarch (*Cakkavatti*):

Just as assemblies are delighted by the sight and speech of a Cakkavatti, the fourfold assembly (monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen) was delighted merely by seeing Ven. Ānanda and hearing him teach. He exercised restraint by pausing while listeners still longed for more Dhamma.

6. The Sixfold Secret of Great Learning (*Bahussuta*):

As analyzed by Ven. Sāriputta, true learning requires: mastering the nine-fold Buddha-word (navaṅga-buddhasāsana), expounding it in detail, teaching it to others, reciting it regularly, pondering its meaning in the mind, and actively consulting learned elders during rainy retreats.

7. Arahantship Free from the Four Postures:

Ven. Ānanda’s attainment of Arahantship occurred outside the four standard physical postures (walking, standing, sitting, lying down)—specifically during the momentary transition when reclining at night. This highlights the potency of continuous mindfulness of the body (kāyagatāsati) combined with determination (ṭhiti).

8. Psychological Purpose of Rituals and Buddha-Pūjā:

Devotional acts (such as stupa worship and venerating the Ānanda Bodhi tree) are symbolic continuations of Ven. Ānanda's service. Their true objective is to generate mental clarity (cetaso pasāda) and faith (saddhā), functioning like the water-clearing gem (udaka-pasādaka maṇi) to settle the mud of mental hindrances (nīvaraṇa) and enable concentration (samādhi).

9. The Structural Evolution of Spiritual Faculties (*Indriya*) into Powers (*Bala*):

Faith (saddhā) acts as the foundational seed and ruling faculty that dispels doubt (vicikicchā) and activates unshakeable resolve (adhimokkha). This launching of faith sequentially drives right effort (viriya), establishes mindfulness (sati), unifies the mind in concentration (samādhi), and culminates in liberating wisdom (paññā) regarding Dependent Arising (paṭiccasamuppāda).

10. Preservation of the Sāsana through Meaning (*Attha*) and Phrasing (*Byañjana*):

The longevity of the Buddha’s Dispensation depends on retaining both the exact wording/phrasing (byañjana) and the accurate underlying meaning (attha) of the suttas. Altering the phrasing distorts the meaning, accelerating the decline of the Dhamma. Reciting "Evaṁ me sutaṁ" ("Thus have I heard") serves as a historical tribute to Ven. Ānanda's role in preserving this textual precision.
