Sermon 083 Summary

Pahankanuwa Sermon #83 Summary

1. Overview

Sermon Title / Number: Pahankanuwa Sermon #83

Primary Theme: The essential necessity of personal effort (viriya) and spiritual urgency (saṁvega) in conquering the internal army of defilements (kilesa-māra) through the practical implementation of the Thirty-Seven Factors of Awakening (bodhipakkhiya-dhamma).

2. Background Story (Nidāna-Kathā)

The Protagonist: A ruthless hunter living near the Gāminda vāla monastery in Ruhuna, ancient Sri Lanka, who later was ordained as Venerable Milakkhatissa ("Milakkha" denoting a barbarian/uncivilized person).

The Interaction: After butchering and eating a trapped deer in the forest, the hunter experienced an unquenchable, excruciating thirst. He entered the nearby monastery to drink water. However, due to his intense bad karma, the full water pots appeared completely empty to him. Venerable Cūla Piṇḍapātika Tissa observed the man shouting and realized he was acting as a living hungry ghost (peta). Out of compassion, the Elder poured ten pots of water into his hands, but the water felt like liquid poured onto a red-hot iron pan, leaving his thirst unquenched.

The Disaster / Turning Point: The Elder warned the hunter that he was experiencing the retribution of a peta in his present life and asked him to contemplate the unimaginable tortures of hell. Struck with intense spiritual urgency (saṁvega), the hunter immediately ran home, dismantled his traps, released his captive animals, destroyed his weapons, and begged the Elder for monastic ordination. Later, after hearing the Devadūta Sutta and witnessing a psychic demonstration of a spark from the Avīci hell instantly reducing green firewood to ashes, his fear of saṃsāra intensified even further.

The Result / Resolution: Ordained as Ven. Milakkhatissa, he devoted himself to intense, uncompromising effort (viriya). To battle sleepiness during night meditation across various shrines (Sithulpawwa, Gāminda vāla, and Kiri Vehera), he placed a wet straw ring on his head and kept his feet in cold water. Upon hearing a novice monk recite the Ārabhatha verse ("Exert yourselves! Go forth! Engage in the Buddha's Dispensation..."), he reflected that the sermon was delivered directly for an energetic practitioner like himself. Spurred by deep spiritual rapture, he attained absorptions (jhāna), reached Non-returning (anāgāmī-phala), and subsequently realized full Arahantship.

3. Core Dhamma Points Emphasized

1. The Threefold Elements of Energetic Initiative (Viriyavāda):

The Buddha defines human capability through three fundamental elements (dhātus): the element of initiative (ārambha-dhātu), the element of launching (nikkama-dhātu), and the element of exertion/valour (parakkama-dhātu). These elements refute passive karma-determinism and highlight the individual's inherent power to break the round of suffering (dukkha).

2. The Tenfold Army of Māra (Māra-Senā / Kilesa-Māra):

As outlined in the Padhāna Sutta, the internal army of defilements consists of ten forces:

1. Sensual pleasures (kāmā),

2. Discontent/aversion to spiritual life (arati),

3. Hunger and thirst (khuppipāsā),

4. Craving (taṇhā),

5. Sloth and torpor (thīna-middha),

6. Fear (bhiru),

7. Doubt/indecision (vicikicchā),

8. Disparagement and obstinacy (makkha-thambha),

9. Gain, honor, and renown (lābha-sakkāra-siloka), and

10. Self-exaltation and disparaging others (attukkaṁsana-paravambhana).

3. The Thirty-Seven Factors of Awakening (Bodhipakkhiya-Dhamma) as Counter-Army:

The counter-offensive against Māra is structured into seven spiritual battalions: the Four Establishments of Mindfulness (satipaṭṭhāna), Four Right Efforts (sammappadhāna), Four Bases of Spiritual Power (iddhipāda), Five Faculties (indriya), Five Powers (bala), Seven Factors of Awakening (bojjhaṅgā), and the Noble Eightfold Path (ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo).

4. The Dynamics of the Four Right Efforts (Sammappadhāna):

Right effort operates through a fivefold mental application: generating desire (chandaṁ janeti), making an effort (vāyamati), arousing energy (viriyaṁ ārabhati), exerting the mind (cittaṁ paggaṇhāti), and striving (padahati). This applies to unarisen unwholesome states, arisen unwholesome states, unarisen wholesome states, and arisen wholesome states.

5. The Five Faculties (Indriya) and Powers (Bala):

Realization of Nibbāna does not occur through the five physical senses, but through the development of spiritual faculties: faith (saddhā), energy (viriya), mindfulness (sati), concentration (samādhi), and wisdom (paññā). When actively deployed against defilements, these faculties function as the Five Powers (pañca balāni).

6. The Integrated Supramundane Path-Moment and its Guarding Triad:

In the supramundane path-moment (magga-citta), all eight path factors arise simultaneously in an instant of "immediate concentration" (ānantarika-samādhi). During this moment, three factors provide functional synergy: Right View (sammā-diṭṭhi) leads in front, Right Effort (sammā-vāyāma) pushes from behind providing momentum, and Right Mindfulness (sammā-sati) stands close at hand supplying protective attendance.

7. Critique of Devotional Passivity (Bhaktivāda vs. Vīriyavāda):

Ven. Nanananda Thero critiques the modern view that Nibbāna is obsolete or unreachable in this era. Replacing energetic effort (vīriyavāda) with a devotional cult of passivity (bhaktivāda) contradicts the Buddha's explicit instruction: "Tumhehi kiccaṁ ātappaṁ, akkhātāro tathāgatā" ("You yourselves must make the effort; the Tathāgatas are only teachers").

8. Seizing the Precious Moment (Khaṇa-Sampatti):

The world operates on time, but true temporal value lies in khaṇa-sampatti—the rare coincidence of human birth free from the eight unfortunate circumstances (attha akkhana) during the presence of a Buddha's Dispensation. Missing this moment leads to severe rebirth in hell (Khaṇātītā hi socanti). The practitioner must adopt the motto: "Khaṇo ve mā upaccagā" ("Let not the precious moment pass you by!").

9. Bases of Spiritual Urgency (Saṁvega-Vatthu) and Saṃsāric Extent:

Spiritual urgency acts as the fuel and velocity for liberation. Reflecting on the eight bases of urgency—birth, aging, illness, death, past suffering, future suffering, and the toil of daily survival—reawakens energy. As highlighted in the Anamatagga Sutta, a single individual's accumulated bones in one eon exceed Mount Vepulla, and their shed blood and tears exceed the ocean waters.

10. Correcting Mental Perversions (Vipallāsa) via Wise Attention (Yoniso Manasikāra):

Unenlightened beings look at the world through four distorted perversions (vipallāsa): taking the impermanent (anicca) as permanent, the unbeautiful (asubha) as beautiful, the painful (dukkha) as pleasant, and non-self (anattā) as self. Liberation requires "the voice of another" (paratoghosa) combined with wise attention (yoniso manasikāra) to align perception with the Three Characteristics (ti-lakkhaṇa).

11. The Radical Transformation of Stream-Entry (Sotāpatti):

Stream-entry is not merely keeping the five precepts or acquiring theoretical learning (pariyatti). It is a profound, instantaneous internal breakthrough (paṭivedha) that directly penetrates dependent co-arising (paṭiccasamuppāda) and releases attachment to the five aggregates (pañcakkhandhā), destroying the vast ocean of saṃsāric suffering (illustrated by the fingernail soil vs. earth soil simile).
