Sermon 059 Summary

Pahankanuwa Sermon #59 Summary

Here is the structured scholarly analysis and summary of Pahankanuwa Dhamma Sermon #59, based on the provided text.

---

Sermon #59 Summary

1. Overview

- Sermon Title / Number: Pahankanuwa Sermon #59

- Primary Theme: The profound doctrinal meaning (attha rasa) of the Ratana Sutta as an Act of Truth (Saccakiriya), focusing on the nature of Arahantship, the cessation of kamma, consciousness without establishing in Name-and-Form (anidassana-viññāṇa), and final extinguishment (nibbāna).

---

2. Background Story (Nidāna-Kathā)

- The Protagonist: The Licchavi kings and citizens of the republic of Vesālī, King Bimbisāra of Rājagaha, Venerable Ānanda, and the Buddha (Tathāgata).

- The Interaction: Facing a catastrophic crisis, the Licchavi rulers gathered in their assembly hall (santhāgāra) and decided to seek refuge in the newly arisen Buddha. They sent envoys with gifts to King Bimbisāra in Rājagaha, where the Buddha was staying at the Veḷuvana monastery. With King Bimbisāra's consent, the envoys invited the Buddha, who accepted out of great compassion.

- The Disaster / Turning Point: Vesālī was stricken by a severe drought leading to a catastrophic threefold fear (tividha bhaya): severe famine, non-human spirits (amanussa) drawn to the stench of unburied corpses thrown outside the city walls, and a deadly pestilence/epidemic (ahivātaka-roga, akin to the plague).

- The Result / Resolution: Upon the Buddha’s arrival at the border of Vesālī, a torrential rainstorm washed away all decaying corpses into the river. Standing at the city gate, the Buddha taught the Ratana Sutta to Venerable Ānanda, instructing him to walk through the city walls reciting it as a protective chant (paritta). This Act of Truth completely dispelled the threefold fear and restored health, peace, and protection to Vesālī.

---

3. Core Dhamma Points Emphasized

1. Meaning over Melody (*Attha Rasa* vs. *Sadda Rasa*):

The efficacy of a protective chant (paritta) relies on it being an authentic Act of Truth (Saccakiriya), which requires a clear understanding of its profound doctrinal meaning (attha rasa). Overemphasizing musical melody (sadda rasa) or relying on automated recordings reduces these sacred chants to hollow sounds and erases the transformative power of the teachings.

2. Etymology and Purpose of Paritta (*Pari-trāṇa*):

Paritta derives from pari ("around" or "from all sides") and trāṇa ("protection"). It signifies comprehensive protection against both seen and unseen dangers. Discourses like the Dhajagga Paritta were taught specifically for forest-dwelling meditators facing fear, trepidation, or hair-standing-on-end (bhayaṃ vā chambhitattaṃ vā lomahaṃso vā) to restore mental steadiness by recollecting the supreme qualities of the Buddha, Dhamma, and Saṅgha.

3. Supremacy of Faith in the Triple Gem (*Aggappasāda Sutta*):

Placing confidence in the supreme—the Buddha among all beings, the Noble Eightfold Path among all prepared things (saṅkhata), Dispassion/Nibbāna (virāga) among all prepared or unprepared things (asaṅkhata), and the Saṅgha among all assemblies—yields supreme spiritual results (aggassa kho panappasannānaṃ, aggo vipāko hoti).

4. The Immediate Nature of Path-Concentration (*Ānantarika-Samādhi*):

The pure concentration praised by the Buddha is the Supramundane Path Concentration (magga-samādhi). It is termed ānantarika ("immediate") because, within a single mind-moment, the eight path factors arise, and the Path is immediately followed by its Fruition (phala) without any temporal interval.

5. Free Extinguishment (*Laddhā Mudhā Nibbutiṃ*):

Sensual coolings in the worldly sense require material expenditure and acquisition. In contrast, the extinguishment enjoyed by Arahants is obtained "for free" (mudhā) through total letting go. They experience this unconditioned peace via the Attainment of the Fruition of Arahantship (arahatta-phala-samāpatti).

6. The Unshakable Noble Disciple (*Indakhīla*) and Stream-Enterers (*Pamāda-Sekha*):

The Stream-enterer (sotāpanna) is compared to a city-pillar (indakhīla), completely unshaken by worldly winds. Even if a Stream-enterer becomes negligent (pamāda-sekha) and fails to attain higher paths in that very life, they are absolutely bound for liberation within a maximum of seven births (sattakkhattuparama), with others taking two or three births (kolaṅkola) or just one birth (ekabīji), never taking an eighth becoming (na te bhavaṃ aṭṭhamaṃ ādiyanti).

7. Instant Abandoning of Three Fetters (*Saṃyojana*) and Incapability (*Abhabbatā*):

Upon achieving the vision of Stream-entry (dassanasampadā), three fetters are simultaneously eradicated: self-illusion (sakkāya-diṭṭhi), doubt (vicikicchā), and attachment to rules and vows (sīlabbata-parāmāsa). Furthermore, the Stream-enterer becomes completely incapable (abhabbo) of committing the six heinous crimes (abhīṭhāna) or concealing any minor wrongdoings.

8. Analytical Breakdown of Old and New Kamma (*Nava Purāṇa Kamma*):

Old Kamma (purāṇa kamma) refers to the six sense faculties (e.g., the eye: abhisaṅkhataṃ, abhisañcetayitaṃ, vedanīyaṃ daṭṭhabbaṃ—prepared, willed, and to be felt). Both the Buddha and Arahants must undergo the felt results of past kamma through their physical senses until the final moment of anupādisesa-nibbāna-dhātu. New Kamma (nava kamma) has no new arising (navaṃ natthi sambhavaṃ) for Arahants because ignorance and craving are completely eradicated.

9. The Triad of Rebirth and Destruction of the Seed (*Khīṇabījā*):

Rebirth requires three conditions: Kamma is the field (kammaṃ khettaṃ), consciousness is the seed (viññāṇaṃ bījaṃ), and craving is the moisture (taṇhā sineho). Arahants are "those with seeds destroyed" (khīṇabījā) because the core of the consciousness seed—Name-and-Form (nāmarūpa)—has been extracted through penetrating insight.

10. Non-Manifestative Consciousness (*Anidassana-Viññāṇa*):

The liberated mind of an Arahant is anidassana-viññāṇa—a non-manifestative, unestablished consciousness that does not land or establish in Name-and-Form (nāmarūpa). It represents the true cessation of consciousness (viññāṇa-nirodha), fully manifested at the moment of final passing away (parinibbāna).

11. Eradication of Subtle Desire (*Avirūḷhichandā*) & Extinguishment Like a Lamp:

Chanda (subtle desire/will) acts as the hidden shoot or sprout of craving. Because Arahants have destroyed kamma and severed attachment to Name-and-Form, no new desire can sprout (avirūḷhichandā). Without further oil or wicks added, these wise ones (dhīrā) undergo final extinguishment just like a lamp going out (nibbanti dhīrā yathāyaṃ padīpo).

12. Present-Life Experience of the Deathless (*Amata*):

Nibbāna is called the Deathless (amata) because even a glimpse of it frees one from the fear of death. By letting go of grasping (upādāna), the practitioner directly experiences this extraordinary supramundane peace in this very life (sandiṭṭhiko dhammo), long before the physical dissolution of the body.
