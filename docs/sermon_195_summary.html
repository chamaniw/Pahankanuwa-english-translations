Sermon 195 Summary

Pahankanuwa Sermon #195 Summary

1. Overview

Sermon Title / Number: Pahankanuwa Sermon #195 (Paṭicca Samuppāda Discourse 13)

Primary Theme: An in-depth structural analysis of saṅkhāra (preparations/fabrications) across the Law of Dependent Arising (Paṭicca Samuppāda) and the Five Aggregates of Grasping (pañcupādānakkhandhā). Ven. Katukurunde Nanananda Thero highlights the subtle layers of underlying physical, verbal, and mental preparations (kāya-, vacī-, citta-saṅkhāra) as the "invisible rehearsal for the drama of becoming," exposes the deceptive/constructed nature of all conditioned phenomena (saṅkhata), and clarifies that Nibbāna (asaṅkhata) is not a concrete "thing" or realm, but the total stilling and cessation of all preparations (sabbasaṅkhārasamatha).

2. Background Story (Nidāna-Kathā)

The Protagonist: The lay disciple Visākha in dialogue with the Arahant Therī Dhammadinnā (from the Cūḷavedalla Sutta, MN 44), as well as contemporary non-Buddhist ascetics/yogins (from the Poṭṭhapāda Sutta, DN 9, and Pañcattaya Sutta, MN 102).

The Interaction: Visākha questions Arahant Therī Dhammadinnā regarding the precise definitions and operational mechanisms of physical preparation (kāya-saṅkhāra), verbal preparation (vacī-saṅkhāra), and mental preparation (citta-saṅkhāra). In parallel, ancient ascetics query the Buddha about the ultimate stage of perceptual cessation (abhisaññā-nirodha).

The Disaster / Turning Point: Non-Buddhist meditators, though capable of attaining high absorption states (jhāna), were trapped in an endless "game of see-saw." They recognized that perception is a disease (saññā rogo) and a dart, yet feared that total absence of perception was delusion (asaññā sammoho). Lacking insight into the underlying background of ignorance (avijjā) and the deceptive nature of fabrications (saṅkhāra), they mistook subtle states like "nothingness" (ākiñcaññāyatana / natthi kiñci) for an actual substantial "thing" or eternal sphere, thereby spinning endlessly in Saṁsāra.

The Result / Resolution: Arahant Therī Dhammadinnā demonstrates how breathing, deliberation, and feeling/perception function as hidden preparations. The Buddha reveals the true exit: saññā-vedayita-nirodha (cessation of perception and feeling) attained through the pacification of intention (cetanā). He demonstrates that Nibbāna is not an existent object (dhamma) or place, but the unconditioned (asaṅkhata) cessation of all fabrications—signless (animitta), undirected (appaṇihita), and void (suññatā).

3. Core Dhamma Points Emphasized

1. Dual Contemplation of Saṅkhāra (Dvayatānupassanā)

Using the Dvayatānupassanā Sutta (Sutta Nipāta), the sermon establishes the fundamental duality: whatever suffering arises is entirely dependent on preparations (sabbaṁ saṅkhāra paccayā); with the total detachment and cessation of preparations, suffering ceases (saṅkhārānaṁ nirodhena natthi dukkhassa sambhavo).

2. The Three Fundamental Saṅkhāras as the "Rehearsal for Becoming"

Drawing from the Cūḷavedalla Sutta, the three underlying preparations are defined in the singular:

Kāya-saṅkhāra (bodily preparation): Inhalation and exhalation (assāsa-passāsā).

Vacī-saṅkhāra (verbal preparation): Reasoning and deliberation (vitakka-vicāra).

Citta-saṅkhāra (mental preparation): Perception and feeling (saññā ca vedanā ca).

Ven. Nanananda uses the Iceberg Simile: visible wholesome/unwholesome actions are only 1/10th above water; the hidden 9/10ths consist of these subtle preparations acting as an invisible "rehearsal" or stage-decoration for the drama of becoming (bhava).

3. Progressive Pacification (Passaddhi) across the Jhānas

The Buddha outlines six stages of pacification:

Speech is pacified in the 1st Jhāna.

Reasoning/deliberation (vitakka-vicāra) in the 2nd Jhāna.

Rapture/joy (pīti) in the 3rd Jhāna.

Respiration (assāsa-passāsā) in the 4th Jhāna.

Perception and feeling (saññā ca vedanā) in the attainment of cessation (nirodha-samāpatti).

Lust, anger, and delusion (rāga, dosa, moha) in the Arahant whose influxes are extinct (khīṇāsava).

4. Reframing "Nothingness" and the Traps of Reification

Non-Buddhist ascetics misconstrued the state of natthi kiñci ("there is nothing") by objectifying "nothingness" as a real state or place, thereby creating a samāpatti that led to prolonged re-manifestation in higher Brahma worlds. True liberation occurs in the Poṭṭhapāda Sutta context when the meditator discerns that even intentionalizing (cetayamāna) is gross, relinquishes cetanā, and steps beyond perception into saññā-vedayita-nirodha.

5. Structural Shift: Subtle Saṅkhāra vs. Resultant Saṅkhārā in the Aggregates

A vital distinction is made between the underlying process saṅkhāra (singular, second link of Paṭicca Samuppāda) and saṅkhārā (plural, fourth aggregate of pañcupādānakkhandhā):

In the aggregates, saṅkhārā is explicitly defined as the six classes of intention (chayime cetanākāyā): intentions rooted in form, sound, smell, taste, touch, and mental objects.

Saṅkhata-saṅkhārā refers to "prepared preparations"—the complex outcome of the mutual interplay between consciousness (viññāṇa) and name-and-form (nāmarūpa).

6. The Deceptive Nature of the Five Aggregates (Khajjanīya Sutta)

The five aggregates of grasping are fundamentally deceptive fabrications (saṅkhata):

Form (rūpa) = A mass of foam (pheṇapiṇḍa); defined as ruppati (that which is oppressed/afflicted like a boil by heat, cold, hunger).

Feeling (vedanā) = A water bubble (bubbuḷa).

Perception (saññā) = A mirage (marīcikā).

Preparations (saṅkhārā) = A plantain/banana trunk (kadallikkhandha), devoid of a core upon peeling.

Consciousness (viññāṇa) = A magical illusion (māyā).

7. Etymological Depth of Abhisaṅkhāra and the Three Kinetic Forces

Abhisaṅkhāra denotes a "special preparing" characterized by kinetic momentum, urgency, or impatience (illustrated by the carpenter's spinning wheel in the Pacetana Sutta). In the context of Kamma, it drives dynamic continuity:

Puññābhisaṅkhāra: Preparatory force generating fortunate rebirths (human/deva realms).

Apuññābhisaṅkhāra: Force preparing rebirth in woeful states (hell/animal realms).

Āneñjābhisaṅkhāra: Unshakable force associated with formless jhānas. Illustrated by the Spinning Top Simile—the top appears stationary and erect, yet is spinning rapidly, capturing how beings remain locked in long-lived formless existences through sheer meditative momentum.

8. Shift from Citta-saṅkhāra to Mano-saṅkhāra in Kamma Accumulation

Subtle mental preparations (citta-saṅkhāra = saññā + vedanā) do not in themselves accumulate kamma. However, when sense objects (dhammā) reaching the mind are grasped as substantial "things" due to underlying craving (taṇhā) and ignorance (avijjā), they escalate into mano-saṅkhāra (volitional mental preparations/intentions), driving the kamma mechanism described in the opening verses of the Dhammapada (Mano pubbaṅgamā dhammā...).

9. De-reification of Asaṅkhata (The Unconditioned / Nibbāna)

Ven. Nanananda strongly warns against misinterpreting Asaṅkhata (or Asaṅkhata-dhātu) as an absolute, objective "thing" or meta-realm. Saṅkhata means "prepared/fabricated"; Asaṅkhata means the absence/cessation of preparation. It is defined by three negative characteristics (asaṅkhatalakkhaṇāni): no arising is manifest (na uppādo paññāyati), no passing away is manifest (na vayo paññāyati), and no change in the static is manifest (na ṭhitassa aññathattam paññāyati).

10. Re-evaluating the Funeral Stanza: *Aniccā vata saṅkhārā*

The popular funeral verse (Aniccā vata saṅkhārā / uppādavayadhammino...) is reclaimed from ritualistic conventionality and restored to its status as an operational summary of core Dhamma: fabrications are inherently impermanent and subject to arising and ceasing; their complete pacification (tesaṁ vūpasamo) is true supreme bliss (sukho). Liberation (visaṅkhāragataṁ cittaṁ) is the mind emptied of, and transcending, all preparatory momentum.
