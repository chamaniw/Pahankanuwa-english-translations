Sermon 150 Summary

Pahankanuwa Sermon #150 Summary

1. Overview

Sermon Title / Number: Pahankanuwa Sermon #150 (Pahankaṇuwa Sinhala Audio Dhamma Sermon No.129)

Primary Theme: The supreme, victorious nature of the Dhamma over all material gifts, tastes, delights, and suffering; the analytical dismantling of craving (taṇhā) as the "seamstress" (sibbanī) that binds beings to saṁsāric existence; and the realization of liberation (vimutti) by understanding the two extremes and the middle path of cessation (nirodha).

2. Background Story (Nidāna-Kathā)

The Protagonist: Ven. Ānanda Thera and an unnamed Buddhist nun residing in Kosambī.

The Interaction: The nun developed a romantic attraction towards Ven. Ānanda. Feigning severe illness, she dispatched a messenger to Ghositārāma asking Ven. Ānanda to visit her nunnery out of compassion and preach the Dhamma to her.

The Disaster / Turning Point: Upon arrival, Ven. Ānanda immediately discerned her covert sensual motives. Instead of addressing her affection directly, he delivered a profound exposition on four foundational factors regarding the body:

1. Āhārasambhūto ayaṁ kāyo...: The body is born of food; yet depending on food, food-craving is to be abandoned through wise reflection (yoniso manasikāra).

2. Taṇhāsambhūto ayaṁ kāyo...: The body is born of craving; yet depending on wholesome desire/craving (e.g., aspiring to attain Arahantship like others), craving is abandoned.

3. Mānasambhūto ayaṁ kāyo...: The body is born of conceit; yet depending on healthy conceit ("If others can attain liberation, so can I!"), conceit is overcome.

4. Methunasambhūto ayaṁ kāyo...: The body is born of sexual intercourse; but regarding sexual union, the Buddha declared absolute bridge-destruction (setughāto)—complete disconnection.

The Result / Resolution: Realizing her manipulative error and deeply shaken by Ven. Ānanda’s clear Dhamma teaching, the nun immediately arose from her couch, prostrated at Ven. Ānanda’s feet, openly confessed her foolishness and unwholesome intent, and begged for forgiveness, restoring her spiritual integrity.

3. Core Dhamma Points Emphasized

1. The Fourfold Supremacy of Dhamma (Dhammapada 354)

The Buddha declared that the gift of Dhamma (dhammadāna) excels all gifts; the taste of Dhamma (dhammarasa) excels all tastes; delight in Dhamma (dhammarati) excels all delights; and the destruction of craving (taṇhakkhaya) conquers all suffering (sabbadukkha).

2. Superiority of Spiritual Over Material Favors

Comparing material things (āmisa) to the Dhamma across three categories—Giving (dāna vs. dhammadāna), Sharing (saṃvibhāga), and Helping (anuggaha)—the Dhamma is consistently declared supreme. Even a single four-line verse (gāthā) of Dhamma that leads to Stream-entry (sotāpatti)—such as Ven. Assaji’s half-verse to Upatissa—yields far greater fruit (mahatphala mahānisaṃsa) than vast material offerings.

3. The Taste of Liberation (Vimutti-rasa) and the Deathless (Amata)

Just as the ocean has only one taste (salt), the Dhamma-Vinaya has only one taste: liberation (vimutti-rasa). Experiencing the arising and passing away of the five aggregates (udayabbaya) generates a taste of joy and gladness (pīti-pāmojja) that is identical to the Deathless (amata), far surpassing fleeting tongue-tastes or celestial nectar.

4. Superhuman Delight (Amānusī Rati) vs. Boredom (Arati)

Sensual delight (kāma-rati) must be abandoned, whereas spiritual delight (abhirati) supports monastic life. When an insight meditator abides in a secluded place (suññāgāra) with a calm mind, a noble "superhuman delight" (amānusī rati) arises, eclipsing all worldly attachments and generating immense spiritual buoyancy (pīti).

5. Craving as an Unquenchable Thirst (Pipāsa)

Sensual gratification (assāda) misleads worldlings into viewing craving (taṇhā) as beneficial. However, craving is fundamentally an unquenchable thirst (pipāsa). Nibbāna is described as pipāsa-vinayo—the total appeasing and subduing of thirst, which puts an end to all grief and fear (soko / bhaya).

6. Doctrinal Similes for Craving (Taṇhā)

The text highlights various scriptural metaphors describing craving's destructive, binding nature:

Tree Root: Re-sprouting suffering as long as the root remains uncut.

Māluvā Creeper: A parasitic vine that smothers its host tree.

Resin / Glue (Lepa / Ālepa): Sticky adhesion to existence.

Moisture (Sineha): Moisture nourishing the seed of consciousness (viññāṇa) in the field of karma.

Endless Torrent (Taṇhā-sota): The infinite river of saṁsāric desire (Natthi taṇhāsamā nadī).

7. Constructive Use of Desire and Conceit (Taṇhaṃ/Mānaṃ Nissāya Pahātabbo)

Desire and conceit can be skillfully directed toward spiritual progress—using the desire/conceit to equal or emulate an Arahant's attainment to propel oneself to Nibbāna. Once the goal is reached, the scaffold of desire and conceit is relinquished. Conversely, sexual activity (methuna) allows no subtle application; it requires absolute destruction of the bridge (setughāto).

8. Craving as the Seamstress (Sibbanī)

In the Metteyya-pañha (Sutta Nipāta), the Buddha calls craving the "seamstress" (sibbanī). Craving repeatedly sews together renewed existence (ponobhavikā), bound up with delight and lust (nandirāgasahagatā tatratatrābhinandinī), stitching one birth to another across saṁsāra.

9. The Six Thera Interpretations of the "Two Extremes and the Middle"

In the Isipatana Deer Park conference, six elder monks analyzed the verse: "Yo ubhonte viditvāna, majjhe mantā na lippati..." The Buddha validated all six as expositionally correct (pariyāyena subhāsitaṃ), while reaffirming his original intent:

1st Interpretation (Buddha's intended meaning): Extreme 1 = Contact (phassa); Extreme 2 = Arising of contact (phassa-samudaya); Middle = Cessation of contact (phassa-nirodha); Seamstress = Craving (taṇhā).

2nd: Past vs. Future; Middle = Present.

3rd: Pleasant feeling vs. Painful feeling; Middle = Neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling (upekkhā).

4th: Name (nāma) vs. Form (rūpa); Middle = Consciousness (viññāṇa).

5th: 6 Internal sense-bases (ajjhattika āyatana) vs. 6 External sense-bases (bāhira āyatana); Middle = Consciousness (viññāṇa).

6th: Identity (sakkāya) vs. Arising of identity (sakkāya-samudaya); Middle = Cessation of identity (sakkāya-nirodha).

10. The Mechanics of Conceptual Illusions: Mirror and Mirage Similes

Name-and-Form (Nāmarūpa): Compared to a blind man carrying a lame man. Consciousness (viññāṇa) acts like reflecting water on a bridge: an unwise dog sees its reflection and reacts to a false "water dog" (unradical attention, ayoniso manasikāra).

Sense-Bases (Āyatana): Perceptions (saññā) act like a mirage (mṛgathṛṣṇikā). Thirst (craving) drives the deer to chase reflected light, unaware that the internal eye and middle consciousness construct the illusion.

11. The Sewing Machine Analogy & Knowledge of Dissolution (Bhaṅga-Ñāṇa)

Craving acts like a sewing machine's bobbin under-thread, creating knots of grasping (upādāna) that stitch identity together. Through intense contemplation of passing away (Phussa phussa vayaṁ passaṁ) and the knowledge of dissolution (bhaṅga-ñāṇa), the meditator runs out the under-thread. Though the needle continues moving, no stitches form; existence is severed, resulting in liberation beyond space and time.

12. The Heartwood of the Dhamma: Unshakeable Liberation (Akuppā Cetovimutti)

The ultimate essence (sāro) of the Teaching is unshakeable liberation of mind (akuppā cetovimutti). As declared in the Bāhiya Sutta, in true liberation there is neither "here," nor "there," nor "in-between" (N'ev'idha na huraṁ na ubhayantare). Realizing non-self (anattā) and the total cessation of suffering (dukkha-nirodha) culminates in the horizonless freedom of Arahantship fruition (arahattaphala-samāpatti).
